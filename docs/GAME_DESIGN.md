# Industrial Warfare — Game Design

## High-Level Concept

Industrial Warfare is a top-down 3D factory automation and industrial combat game.

The player lands on hostile worlds, gathers resources, builds automated production systems, expands industrial infrastructure, and defends that infrastructure from enemy factions that vary by planet.

The game combines:

- Factory automation
- Logistics
- Resource extraction
- Exploration
- Base building
- Defensive warfare
- Abandoned settlements and cities
- Planet-specific enemy factions

---

## Core Design Pillars

### 1. Build

The player should be able to transform an undeveloped area into a functioning industrial base.

This includes:

- Resource extraction
- Processing
- Manufacturing
- Storage
- Logistics
- Power
- Defensive infrastructure

The factory itself should become one of the player's primary tools for survival.

### 2. Automate

Manual actions should gradually become automated.

The player begins with limited production capability and builds increasingly complex systems that reduce repetitive work and increase industrial output.

Automation should feel rewarding because the factory becomes more efficient as the player improves it.

### 3. Expand

The player must explore and expand beyond the starting area to obtain better resources and new strategic locations.

Expansion creates new opportunities but also increases risk.

Larger industrial operations may attract more enemy attention and require larger defensive networks.

### 4. Defend

The world should actively resist industrial expansion.

Enemy factions can attack:

- Players
- Production buildings
- Resource operations
- Logistics routes
- Power infrastructure
- Defensive positions

Defense should be integrated into factory design rather than existing as a completely separate game system.

### 5. Explore

The world should contain more than empty terrain.

Locations may include:

- Abandoned settlements
- Towns
- Small cities
- Industrial ruins
- Resource-rich areas
- Enemy-controlled zones

Exploration should provide resources, information, strategic locations, or danger.

---

## Planet and Enemy Concept

Different planets or environments can contain different enemy factions.

The game should not rely on a single universal enemy type.

Possible enemy factions may include:

- Zombies
- Biological creatures
- Hostile machines
- Rogue military forces
- Alien organisms
- Other planet-specific threats

Each faction should eventually influence how the player builds and defends a factory.

---

## First Prototype — Zombie Colony World

The first major prototype will take place on a world containing abandoned human settlements affected by a zombie outbreak.

The environment may include low-density settlements, towns, industrial locations, and eventually small cities.

Buildings and urban areas can function as hostile population centers.

Zombie-controlled locations may:

- Contain enemies
- Generate enemies
- Protect valuable resources
- Block expansion routes
- Create persistent threats near the factory

The player may eventually choose to clear, contain, bypass, or exploit these areas.

---

## Core Gameplay Loop

The initial gameplay loop is:

Explore → Gather → Build → Automate → Defend → Expand

As the player's factory grows, the loop repeats at a larger scale.

The long-term goal is for factory growth and enemy pressure to interact with each other.

Expansion should create greater production capability while also increasing exposure to danger.

---

## First Night Vertical Slice

The First Night vertical slice is the first target for a complete playable experience.

The player should be able to:

1. Enter the world.
2. Explore the immediate area.
3. Locate basic resources.
4. Establish a small factory.
5. Build a simple production chain.
6. Construct defensive structures.
7. Prepare for an incoming zombie attack.
8. Survive the first major night attack.

The vertical slice does not need every planned feature.

Its purpose is to prove that factory building, automation, exploration, and defense work together as a fun core experience.

---

## Current Development Philosophy

Development will prioritize simple working systems before complexity.

Systems should be:

- Understandable
- Modular
- Testable
- Expandable
- Documented

Temporary placeholder graphics and simplified mechanics are acceptable during early development.

Gameplay functionality is more important than visual polish during the prototype phase.
